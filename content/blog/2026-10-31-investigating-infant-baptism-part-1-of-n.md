It has actually been an year and a half since I wrote part 1. During that time, many things transpired which kept me postponing this second part (part 1 for CS folks).

And during that time, I had also concluded my investigation.

I concluded that infant baptism is not Biblical, and only believing persons having attained an age at which they can reason about or articulate their faith should be baptised.

The above definition "an age at which they can reason about or articulate their faith" is obviously not a technical one, and I am using it in order to avoid using the word "adult" which can also be defined in different ways and is also incorrect.

Anyway, infants should not be baptised and the practice is not Biblical.

As part of my investigation, I had some books (a couple not fully), quite a number of articles, and spent a good deal talking to a theologian who had become a credobaptist from being a pedobaptist. I picked his brain trying to argue from different angles to understand how the argument for pedobaptism is constructed in order to come to a conclusion.

This is the same brother I had mentioned in part 0, who has now become my mentor. I convene with him weekly and learn from him. More about that later.

To be honest, writing a polemical article in order to disprove people I know who espouse pedobaptism was an urge that I had to fight for a long time. And I don't have the mental bandwidth to write such an article like an academic theses. Therefore, the remaining part of this article will mostly be bullet pointers.

- The two books that I primarily read were The Baptism Debate by Peter Goeman, and The Christ Of The Covenants by O. Palmer Robertson.
- I read a booklet written by J. I. Packer titled "An Introduction To Covenant Theology".
- I also read through a bit of the article A String Of Pearls Unstrung by Fred A. Malone (who also has a book), and literature here and there.
- I say here and there because most of them were scattered articles and blog posts on the Internet.
- The basis of the Reformed Presbyterian argument for pedobaptism is Covenant Theology itself.
- This is why a lot of credobaptists fail to effectively argue with pedobaptists.
- The best argument they seem to find is that "it is not in the Bible".
- "It is not in the Bible" is indeed a right argument, however, it is not easily accepted because for a Reformed Presbyterian, it doesn't matter. I'll tell you why.
- The "household" argument is the weakest Reformed Presbyterian argument. I am referring to the argument that children "could have been present" in places the word household was used. This does not prove or disprove anything. In a court of law, such an argument is easily thrown out.
- I eventually understood that in order to attack (theoretically) the RP case for infant baptism, I need to understand their Covenant Theology.
- O. Palmer Robertson's book (and also Goeman's) helped me with that.
- According to RPs, there is in fact only three covenants that are in operation, and all the different covenants in the Bible are "administrations" of these few.
